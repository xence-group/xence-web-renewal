XENCE RESEARCH · No.01 · 2026.11.30

The Potential of Data Centre Waste Heat: Creating Local Value through Low-Temperature Reuse

From heat that is thrown away to heat that is shared. Greenhouses and fish farms can turn a computing facility into a community asset.

Heat field. Each square is drawn at a size proportional to its temperature. Heat once thrown into the air reaches a timber greenhouse and a fish farm within 1 km.

SUMMARY

Purpose　This report sets out how much of a data centre's waste heat can realistically be put to use, and how architecture can turn its low-temperature reuse — in greenhouses, land-based aquaculture, pools and similar uses at roughly 15–35 °C — into lasting value for the surrounding community.

Method　We reviewed policy documents, industry surveys, company announcements and academic papers published up to November 2026, and compared 17 projects that use data centre heat for greenhouses or aquaculture and 10 that upgrade it for district heating by temperature, distance, scale, business model and outcome.

Key findings

  1. The heat is unavoidableAlmost all the electricity a data centre draws ends up as heat. Global consumption is projected to roughly double from about 415 TWh in 2024 to around 950 TWh in 2030, and the same amount of heat is produced every year.
  2. Regulators and communities are asking what happens to itGermany requires new data centres to reuse at least 10% of their energy (ERF), and the EU requires a cost–benefit analysis for sites of 1 MW and above. In Japan, friction over siting is growing.
  3. High-temperature reuse is a proven business in the NordicsHeat pumps raise data centre heat to 70–85 °C for district heating in Odense (215 GWh a year) and elsewhere — but the model depends on heat networks most of Japan does not have.
  4. Direct low-temperature reuse is rare, but its conditions are clearOf 17 projects, three are operating and two are pilots. All five have their heat recipient within 1 km, and three need no heat pump.
  5. Temperature, distance and demand decide successLow-grade heat becomes valuable when supply temperature meets the recipient's need, the recipient sits within 1 km, and demand is steady or tied to high-value produce.
  6. Mid-sized sites of 1–10 MW are a gapProjects cluster at two extremes. The heat recovered from 1 MW of IT load can warm roughly 3,200–7,600 m² of greenhouse, depending on the envelope (our estimate).
  7. Local value takes five formsEnergy and carbon; food and primary industry; jobs and local brands; resilience and public use; public consent and landscape. A nuisance can become a community asset.
