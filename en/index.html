OUR MODEL

The best platform in construction for open-integral architecture.

XENCE designs, builds and operates — not as separate contracts, but as one continuous service. We connect materials across society, from forests, sawmills and demolition sites, turn them into buildings, record how they are used, and pass them on to the next building when their job is done. Open enough to work with anyone, integrated enough to carry a material from source to operation: that is how we turn construction into a circular economy.

Design, build and operation without breaks, with the data fed back into R&D. The more it turns, the wider the material network and the faster and surer the next building.

TECHNOLOGY

Digital tools that bring “every material” up to building standard.

Software we write ourselves links design, fabrication and operation into a single stream of data.

01 Design engine (COMPUTATIONAL DESIGN)

Feed in what’s actually available — lengths, sections, quantities — and the engine generates the structure and every cut. One rule set designs greenhouses, furniture and pavilions.

Irregular stock automatically allocated to frame members

02 Structural analysis (STRUCTURAL ANALYSIS)

Snow and wind loads are run across the full 3D frame, checking deflection and utilisation member by member — the evidence that non-standard material can carry real loads.

Snow and wind load cases on the 3D frame, utilisation per member (illustrative)

03 Digital fabrication (FABRICATION)

We scan irregular stock and shape each member around its curve, grain and waney edge instead of cutting them away. Timber joints lock it together — no steel hardware, fully demountable.

A curved board scanned, and a member shaped to follow its curve

04 3D printing (ADDITIVE MANUFACTURING)

Free-form geometry is stood up, checked for overhangs and automatically reshaped to be printable before slicing. Shown here in steel (WAAM); the same pipeline runs resin and wood composite.

Stood up, checked for overhangs, auto-adjusted to be printable, then built bottom-up (steel WAAM example)

05 Material Passport (TRACEABILITY)

Felling, haulage, sawing, drying, fabrication, building: one QR code records the history, the carbon stored in the wood and the GHG emitted at each step, and passes all three to the piece’s next use. Live since October 2026.

History, stored carbon and GHG emissions, carried by one QR code (sample record)

06 LCA & operating data (WHOLE-LIFE CARBON)

We calculate whole-life carbon with J-CAT and One Click LCA, then pair it with operating data to evidence a building’s value as an asset.

Greenhouse carbon storage, whole-life carbon in J-CAT, and campus facility-cost analytics

MODULAR SYSTEM

From joint to building, scaled in modules.

Our unit of design isn’t the building — it’s the joint. The same connection rules that join irregular timber assemble furniture, greenhouses and architecture, scaled up in software. At every size, design, fabrication and record data stay one continuous thread.

01 — JOINT · 0.1 m

The joint

Sawmill offcuts meet at a metal 3D-printed node, topology-optimised to fit each piece. Unbolt it and everything comes apart for reuse. The base unit of everything.

02 — PRODUCT · 1 m

Furniture & product

The same sawmill offcuts become furniture in a different module. A festival lantern stand reassembles straight into a bench.

03 — GREENHOUSE · 10 m

Greenhouse

Modules link into greenhouses — from 80 m² to several hectares, without changing the rules.

04 — ARCHITECTURE · 30 m

Architecture

Houses are designed on the same joint rules and data, down to every post-and-beam connection. Take one apart and it becomes material for the next building.

WHY IT LASTS

Why XENCE keeps creating value.

Ending scrap-and-build only works if the model itself keeps going. Here are six reasons it does.

01Lifecycle operation

Design data becomes the base for operation and disclosure

From the design stage, XENCE combines the life-cycle CO₂ of every member with the building’s 3D model. That model becomes the base for facility management and non-financial disclosure, so the same data supports operation, maintenance and carbon reporting after handover. Material data, joint fabrication data (Shimoda Flange), operating data (MEMU) and LCA join up — making XENCE not a builder that walks away, but a platform that keeps building data working.

02Parametric design

A design rule made once keeps creating value

The Circular Timber Greenhouse™ is a modular product whose joints and member layout are held as parametric rules. Enter the site and size, and structure, quantities, cost and fabrication data follow automatically, so no project starts from a blank sheet. As volume grows, design and estimating effort per project falls and margins can rise. The rule set itself accumulates as an asset, reaching more places, faster.

03Digital fabrication

It accelerates local making everywhere

Sawmills across Japan already own saws and CNC machines. XENCE outputs design data built for irregular timber, matched to each region’s machines. Existing equipment becomes a tool for higher-value parts, so local manufacturing can start without building new factories.

04Greenhouse market

A market big enough to absorb it

Japan has 37,000 ha under protected cultivation. Converting just 1% (370 ha) to timber greenhouses at ¥40,000 per m² means about ¥148bn of demand. Timber greenhouses can take in the widening range of agricultural buildings, from plant factories to aquaponics.

05LCA & material passports

Regulation now asks for environmental performance in numbers

Under Japan’s revised Building Energy Efficiency Act (July 2026), a building LCA scheme is planned for FY2028, with mandatory filing for offices over 5,000 m². The EU is introducing Digital Product Passports. XENCE already runs J-CAT assessments and Material Passports in practice, so it can deliver the numbers these rules require from day one.

06Platform model

No forests, no factories — growth through partnership

XENCE owns no forests and no mills. It links Japan’s ~3,500 sawmills — a ¥1.4tn precut and sawmilling market — and local builders through inventory and design data. Free of plant investment, it scales quickly through regional partners, on the same system.
